## The occasion

On **26 April 2025**, I joined the China-Latin American Literature Roundtable Dialogue associated with the Consulate of Colombia and Global Times as an invited roundtable speaker.

## My contribution

I delivered a keynote speech on Sino-Latin American literary exchanges and joined roundtable discussions with the Colombian Ambassador on international relations and cultural cooperation.

## What connects

Speaking across cultural contexts asks for more than fluency. It asks for attention to audience, references, and the different experiences people bring to the same conversation.

This is part of my broader interest in bilingual communication, hosting, and visual storytelling.


## Two forms of public expression

The supplied record contains both a keynote speech and roundtable participation. These are related but distinct contributions. An address gives a speaker a developed line of thought to present. A roundtable places that contribution in a conversation with other participants. Keeping both in view gives the event more substance than a single description such as speaker or guest.

The subject was Sino-Latin American literary exchange. The discussion with the Colombian Ambassador extended to international relations and cultural cooperation. This is the documented scope of participation; it does not establish a diplomatic appointment or a professional qualification in translation or literary scholarship.

## Interest, preparation and the public occasion

My interest in literature and languages began before university, alongside interests in cinema, art and traditional theatre. The roundtable is one public setting connected to that background. The relationship does not need a dramatic origin story. An early interest can remain a continuing curiosity while a later occasion provides a specific role and audience.

What makes the case distinctive is the combination of literary content and a cross-cultural setting. A reference that feels familiar to one audience may need context for another. A well-formed argument also has to make space for questions and different perspectives when it enters a group discussion. These are interpretive questions prompted by the occasion, rather than reported quotations from it.

## A question beyond this event

The new language-and-context essay asks what it means to make an idea accessible across languages without flattening the culture around it. Its examples are constructed reading exercises. They are identified as hypothetical, not as private conversations recalled from the salon.

The relationship with my other cases is through audience and form. Health outreach asks how someone encounters an unfamiliar subject. Hosting asks how a gathering follows a live sequence. Literary exchange asks how references and meaning travel in conversation. Looking at their differences is more revealing than treating all three as interchangeable communication experience.
