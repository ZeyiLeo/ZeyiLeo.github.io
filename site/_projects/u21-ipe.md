## The work

Selected to represent The University of Hong Kong, I collaborated with multidisciplinary teams from global universities in the U21 Health Sciences Group International Programme 2026.

My CV records my role as **Group Leader**, with work spanning research, stakeholder engagement, cross-cultural teamwork, and strategic presentations.

## From perspectives to a shared response

The work involved developing evidence-based responses to global healthcare challenges and presenting policy recommendations and project outcomes to an international academic audience.

Working across disciplines makes communication part of the substance of the project: different approaches need to become a clear, shared argument.

## What connects

Healthcare, research, and international communication meet here. The experience brings together evidence, collaboration, and the challenge of presenting a recommendation clearly.


## A shared argument across disciplines

The programme brought together multidisciplinary teams from global universities. My recorded role was Group Leader, representing HKU. The work included research, stakeholder engagement, teamwork and presentations. This combination places the project between examining a question and making a shared response understandable to other people.

A multidisciplinary setting is relevant because the final presentation has to hold more than one perspective together. Research material, a stakeholder concern and a proposed recommendation are not the same kind of statement. Organising their relationship is part of explaining a group response clearly. The case can describe that task without assigning an unrecorded specialty or finding to the team.

## Research and recommendations have different jobs

The supplied CV records evidence-based responses to global healthcare challenges and the presentation of policy recommendations and project outcomes to an international academic audience. Research provides material for a response; a recommendation proposes what should follow from that material. A presentation then gives the audience a route through the argument.

These distinctions are useful when reading the role. Evidence should not disappear behind a polished delivery, and the confidence of a presentation is not itself proof that a recommendation has been adopted.

## Where the conversation continues

The case raises a continuing question about health-sciences communication: how can a group show the relationship between a problem, evidence and a proposed response without presenting a complex issue as settled? That is a useful topic for further reflection rather than a claim that this project resolved every tension involved.
