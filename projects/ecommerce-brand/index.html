An independent e-commerce venture connecting brand building, operations, and data-informed decisions.

Amazon operations · Marketing analytics

From brand to operation

Since January 2025, I have worked on a cross-border e-commerce business serving the North American market. My work covers brand building, USPTO trademark registration, market research, product selection, and the launch of an Amazon store.

The operating loop

I monitor sales, traffic, and conversion data to inform business decisions. Promotional content, store-page design, and feedback analysis form part of the same operating process.

Selected operating metrics include ACoS below 30% and TACoS up to 21%.

What connects

The work combines communication with execution: positioning a product, understanding its audience, coordinating operations, and using data to revise the approach.

Product screenshots, brand identifiers, and operational dashboards remain outside this public case study.

Different stages, different decisions

The work can be read as a sequence of distinct commitments. Market research and product selection concern what to offer and where to compete. Brand building and trademark registration concern the identity around that offer. The store launch turns those choices into an operating presence on Amazon. Continuing analysis then asks what is happening once the store is live. Each stage creates different information to work with.

These are recorded parts of an independent venture, rather than a demonstration store built for this portfolio. The case does not disclose product identifiers, supplier details or account screens. A typographic view of the workstreams gives visitors an overview of the venture.

Content and operations belong in the same picture

Promotional content and a store page are visible expressions of a business. Their usefulness also depends on the less visible operating work around the offer. My experience includes product selection, store entry, monitoring performance and analysing feedback. In the marketing and shipping assistant role, advertising content and data sat alongside shipping and fulfilment responsibilities. That role and the independent founder role overlapped in 2025; the timeline keeps their responsibilities separate.

This makes execution a useful theme for the case. A compelling description, an available product and a fulfilled order are related concerns, but improving one does not automatically prove that the others improved. The portfolio therefore describes the tasks and documented measures separately instead of collapsing them into a broad claim of commercial success.

Reading a number with its boundary

The ACoS and TACoS figures above come from the supplied CV. They are selected operating figures, not a full financial statement or a newly audited result. Amazon defines ACoS as advertising spend divided by advertising revenue. Its own guide also notes that an appropriate level depends on business context. A ratio should be read with the period, attribution and costs that give it meaning. Amazon Ads: understanding ACoS.

The next question is what kinds of information the work required: sales volume, traffic, conversion, advertising and feedback. Understanding the relationship between those observations is a different task from displaying a single attractive percentage.

An operating question for the writing room

The accompanying new essay considers how an operator might choose the next task after launch. It uses a clearly hypothetical example to distinguish an observation, an interpretation and a proposed change. The example examines the structure of a decision rather than reporting a new commercial result.

This case also connects with programme operations. An admissions record and a store observation are very different objects, but both become more useful when their status and context are clear. The shared interest is disciplined execution: knowing what has happened, what remains uncertain and which next action the available information can support.
